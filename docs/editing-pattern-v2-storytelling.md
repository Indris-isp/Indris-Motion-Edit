# Editing Pattern v2: "Visual Storytelling" Podcast Edit

Source: the 5 reference clips in the second Drive folder. They were transcribed with Whisper, cut points were found with ffmpeg scene detection, and I checked a frame every second.
**This is the pattern to use for your videos.** Section 5 is a fill-in prompt template you can send me.

|---|------|--------|--------|------|----------|
| R1 | Entrepreneur / creator advantage ("$120,000 pre-sales") | 9:16, 30fps | 34.4s | 15 | **~2.3s** |
| R2 | Red-light therapy / skin ageing (Dermalux) | 9:16, 30fps | 56.0s | 20 | **~2.6s** |
| R3 | Alcohol gene variant: "Raw vs Final Video" showcase | 9:16 (in frame) | 40.4s | ~20 visual swaps | **~2s** |
| R4/R5 | "Time doesn't wait": pure motion-graphic piece (R4 is the same clip cut short) | 3:4, 30fps | 10.6s | 7 scenes | **~1.5s** |

**How it differs from the first style (Pierre/Jack):**
- **Footage:** v1 is talking head + text. v2 is **talking head ↔ full-screen illustrative visuals**. About 40–50% of the runtime is *not* the speaker.
- **Visual per line:** every key noun or metaphor gets its own **literal visual** (newspaper for "pre-sales", Chevron building for "strike oil", a brain for "prefrontal cortex", a mitochondria render for "mitochondrial").
- **Pacing:** slower and more premium. Shots last 2–3s instead of 1s, and the text style is elegant rather than loud.

## 3. Pattern analysis (the 6 questions)

### 1) Hook selection and shaping
- The first line is always **number + result** or **"surprising fact about YOU"**: "$120,000 in three days", "8% of people have a gene…", "the one thing that impacts your skin the most is…".
- R2 adds a **title banner question** on top for the first ~3s ("Why Your Skin Ages Faster Than You Think?"). It acts as a thumbnail inside the video.
- **Within 2 seconds the hook number is visualised full-screen** (the newspaper with $120,000, the DNA with 8%), so the eye gets a reward straight away.
- The hook is **one complete sentence**. It's never cut mid-thought, and no host intro comes before it.

### 2) How the story plays out
A mini-lesson arc:
1. **Hook claim** (0–5s)
2. **Principle / explanation** (5–15s)
3. **Relatable scenario**: "you wake up and…", "that guy at 3am…", "it's easy to say what are the odds" (15–30s). The viewer sees themselves here.
4. **The why / science or turn** (25–40s)
5. **Payoff line / thesis** spoken last: "being a creator is the most unfair advantage…", "it is for everybody", "that's a genetic predisposition".

The **title/thesis is saved for the end**. The clip builds up to the line you'd quote.

### 3) How the speaker explains
- Concept → plain-language analogy → personal scenario ("prefrontal cortex = the part right behind your forehead").
- Stats get **repeated** as a callback (8% appears twice in R3).
- Contrast pairs: "not what I already know → what I literally can't do", "UV does damage → also beneficial".
- Speaks in second person ("you wake up, you can't remember someone's name").

### 4) What's cut / kept / transitions
- **Cut:** host questions (R1 and R2 have none at all), set-up, filler, repetitions, and any answer that isn't visualisable.
- **Kept:** micro-reactions from the host as **audio only** ("Oh yeah", "Yeah", "Wow" in R3). They prove it's a live conversation without spending a cut on the host.
- **Multi-source stitching (R2):** lines from **3 different speakers or podcasts** are combined into one argument, like a mini-documentary.
- **Transitions:** mostly **hard cuts on the beat of a word**. Between "worlds" (R4/R5) they use camera push-ins or pull-outs, and within graphics a blur-to-sharp reveal. No glitch effects; this style is "premium/clean".

### 5) Interviewer vs guest
- **Host is almost invisible.** R1 and R2 have zero host on-screen. In R3 the host is audio-only, with three one-word reactions.
- The **guest owns 95–100%** of the runtime. The B-roll does the job the host's reaction shots did in style v1.
- If another voice is used, it's a **second expert** (R2) for credibility, not the interviewer.

### 6) Camera and visual rhythm
- **Cut every 2–3s** (vs 1–1.3s in style v1).
- Alternating pattern: **Speaker → Visual → Speaker → Visual…** The speaker is on screen for the emotion and personal words; a visual takes over for every **noun, stat, metaphor or scenario**.
- Speaker framing: mostly one **MCU** angle, with **punch-ins** (digital zoom ~110–120%) to hide jump-cuts, and one **wide** cutaway per clip (R1 at 12s).
- The three visual types:
  1. **Literal B-roll / AI-generated cinematic scenes** (party, bedroom, kitchen at 3am)
  2. **3D science renders** (mitochondria, brain, DNA, cells), slowly rotating or pushing in
  3. **Graphic design scenes** (newspaper, paper on a cutting mat, black text card, picture frame, retro PC). These are **"designed metaphors"**.
- **Text is always integrated into the visual** (behind buildings, printed on ribbons, on a newspaper, on a monitor), never just floated on top.

### Typography and colour system
| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Captions on the speaker | small white sans, 2–4 words, word-by-word pop, centred at chest/mic height |
| Keyword | **2–3× larger**, a different font (serif or heavy sans) and **one accent colour per video** (R1 lime green, R2 maroon/cream serif, R3 gold serif italic) |
| Negative or emphasised word | red ("CAN'T", "EXPOSE") |
| Graphic scenes | big 3D or extruded headline text with a drop shadow |
| Labels | pill boxes and **callout lines with dots** (diagram style) |
| Colour grade | matches the topic: warm beige (beauty), neutral/cool (business), dark navy (science) |
| Persistent elements | name lower-third for the first 2s; optional title banner at the top; CTA or handle |

## 4. Recipe (step by step)
1. Pick a **40–60s segment** where the guest makes **one argument** with at least one number and one relatable scenario.
2. Open on the **number/result sentence**. Optional: a title banner question at the top for 3s.
3. Remove all host questions. Keep the host's one-word reactions as audio only.
4. End on the **thesis/quotable line**.
5. Go through the transcript and mark every **noun, stat, metaphor and "you…" scenario**. Each one gets a visual (B-roll, 3D render, or designed graphic scene), with roughly **45% visuals / 55% speaker**.
6. Cut every **2–3s**, on word beats. Use punch-ins on the speaker to hide jump-cuts, plus one wide angle.
7. Captions: white word-by-word with **one accent keyword** per phrase, in one accent colour for the whole video.
8. Grade consistently, add the lower-third name tag (first 2s), and finish with the handle/CTA.
