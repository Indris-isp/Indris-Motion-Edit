### Scenes

**Scene 1 · Spreadsheet wall (hook)** — 0:00–0:05, frames 0–300
- On screen: dark background. Spreadsheet tabs slide in and tile into a wall: "Football_Summer_v3.xlsx", "Maxes_FINAL_final", "Wk4_Lifts_PRINT", "Soccer_InSeason". Cells full of percentages and crossed-out numbers. Kinetic headline: "120 athletes. 6 teams. One spreadsheet."
- Motion: tabs drop with overshoot, staggered 3 frames apart. Headline word by word; "One spreadsheet." lands last with a small shake on the wall behind it. Headline holds ≥ 1.5 s.
- Camera: slow push-in 100% → 106%.

**Scene 2 · The manual loop (problem)** — 0:05–0:13, frames 300–780
- On screen: three beats of a coach's week, left to right on one long strip:
  1. Coach copies a 1RM from "Maxes" into a percentage formula, cell turns red: "#REF!".
  2. A printer spits out workout sheets; they land on a weight-room clipboard.
  3. Paper sheets come back with handwritten weights, a coffee ring, one sheet missing.
  Text on the strip: "Build. Print. Collect. Re-type." then "Repeat every week."
- Motion: cursor on arcs with click presses; paper sheets flutter in with offset; the missing sheet fades out with a quick ease-in exit.
- Camera: easy-ease pan left → right across the three beats (≈ 2.5 s per beat, full pan no faster than 0.8 s), slight dolly-out at the end so all three are visible behind "Repeat every week."

**Scene 3 · Build it once (fix)** — 0:13–0:21, frames 780–1260
- On screen: spreadsheet wall collapses into the TeamBuildr Strength program builder (Figma SVG), calendar view. Coach picks a template **[confirm template name]**, drags a "Lower Body A" session onto Monday, and assigns it to a group ("Varsity Football"). Sessions fill the week. Text: "Build the program once."
- Motion: wall-to-UI transition (tiles scale down and snap into calendar cells). Cursor drag on an arc, drop with click press. Calendar cells fill with 3-frame offset, never all at once.
- Camera: push to ≈ 130% on the drag-and-drop, then ease back to 100% to show the full week.

**Scene 4 · Athletes log it (fix)** — 0:21–0:29, frames 1260–1740
- On screen: calendar flies off; a phone with the TeamBuildr athlete app and a weight-room tablet **[confirm tablet view UI]** slide in side by side. On the phone, an athlete sees today's workout, enters "Back Squat 3 × 5 @ 275", taps complete. On the tablet, several athletes' rows tick through. Text: "Athletes log it. In the app or on the rack."
- Motion: devices parented to one null, enter together with overshoot. Number keypad presses (1 → 0.96 → 1). Completion checks stagger down the tablet list.
- Camera: slow lateral drift between phone and tablet; micro-push throughout.

**Scene 5 · See it, adjust it (fix)** — 0:29–0:37, frames 1740–2220
- On screen: logged sets stream from the devices into the coach's desktop view: completed workouts for the group and an athlete's max/PR updating **[confirm which report or screen shows this]**. A "New PR" highlight on one athlete: "Back Squat 275 → 285". Coach opens next week and bumps the working percentage on one session. Text: "See every rep. Adjust next week."
- Motion: data dots travel from devices along arcs into the dashboard. PR number counts up over ~1 s, ease-out. Percentage field edit with click press; affected cells in next week ripple-update with offset.
- Camera: pull back from devices to reveal the dashboard, then easy-ease zoom (≈ 120%) into the edited session.

**Scene 6 · Program in sync (payoff)** — 0:37–0:41, frames 2220–2460
- On screen: the loop closes as one clean diagram drawn over the UI: Build → Log → See → Adjust, arrows looping. Text: "No more spreadsheets. One program, always up to date."
- Motion: arrows draw on in sequence (4-frame offset), loop pulses once. Hold the full statement ≥ 1.5 s with a slow drift.
- Camera: slow pull back, full UI and loop in frame.

**Scene 7 · Logo + CTA** — 0:41–0:45, frames 2460–2700
- On screen: UI scales away. TeamBuildr logo builds. Tagline (client to confirm wording). CTA: "Start your free trial · teambuildr.com" **[confirm CTA — site uses "Start Trial"]**.
- Motion: logo build, tagline with 4-frame offset, slow drift to end.
- Camera: gentle push-in.

**Scene 1 · Who's ready? (hook)** — 0:00–0:03, frames 0–180
- On screen: a team roster of silhouettes, all the same grey. Headline: "40 athletes walk in. Who's ready to train?"
- Motion: silhouettes stagger in; headline word by word, held ≥ 1.5 s.
- Camera: slow push-in.

**Scene 2 · Morning check-in (problem → fix)** — 0:03–0:08, frames 180–480
- On screen: athlete app shows a short questionnaire **[confirm questionnaire UI in athlete app]**: sleep, fatigue, soreness, stress, motivation, each a 1–5 tap. Several phones submit in sequence.
- Motion: taps with press; phones enter with 3-frame offset; each submission sends a dot upward.
- Camera: lateral drift across the phones.

**Scene 3 · Load at a glance (fix)** — 0:08–0:13, frames 480–780
- On screen: dots land in the Load Monitoring Dashboard. Team rows fill with sleep / fatigue / stress / motivation values. One athlete sits under the coach's own threshold and stands out **[confirm how the dashboard highlights this — don't claim automatic alerts unless the UI shows them]**. Text: "The whole team's readiness, before warm-up."
- Motion: rows fill with stagger; the highlighted row lifts slightly with overshoot.
- Camera: easy-ease zoom to the highlighted row (≈ 125%).

**Scene 4 · Where it hurts (fix)** — 0:13–0:17, frames 780–1020
- On screen: that athlete's Body Heat Map: left hamstring marked as sore. Small note: "Reported today".
- Motion: body outline draws on, soreness area blooms in (no flashing). Hold ≥ 1.5 s.
- Camera: micro-push on the body map.

**Scene 5 · Adjust + logo (payoff / logo)** — 0:17–0:20, frames 1020–1200
- On screen: coach swaps the athlete's sprint block for a lighter option in their workout. Text: "Adjust before it becomes an injury"* → TeamBuildr AMS logo, CTA.
- Motion: swap with click press; logo build with offset.
- Camera: pull back, gentle push-in on logo.

\* Wording to approve with the client: keep it about informed coaching decisions, not medical or injury-prevention guarantees.
